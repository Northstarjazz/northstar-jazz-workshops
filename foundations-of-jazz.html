A playing-based way into jazz.

Foundations of Jazz is for musicians who want a clearer, more supportive way into the music. You'll work on the core skills that come up in real playing situations: learning tunes, understanding form, building confidence with improvisation, strengthening time feel, and listening closely to the people around you.

The course is designed for musicians who are newer to jazz, returning to playing, or looking for a stronger foundation before joining an ensemble. Many people take Foundations as a complete experience in itself. For those who want to keep going, it's also a natural first step into North Star's ongoing small-group jazz ensembles.

Why this course

What you'll walk away with.

Most musicians who want to play jazz run into the same gap: knowing some theory isn't the same as being able to play it with other people. Foundations is built to close that gap.

Over six sessions, you'll:

  • Learn and play through real jazz repertoire
  • Develop a working feel for form and how chord changes move
  • Start building an improvisational vocabulary you can actually use
  • Strengthen your time feel and your ear for the group
  • Build a foundation to keep growing — into an ensemble, a jam, or your own practice

Your Teachers

Joe Hartnett & Chris Bates.

Joe Hartnett

Saxophone · Co-Founder

Joe is a saxophonist, educator, and Co-Founder of North Star Jazz Workshops. He holds a B.F.A. in Jazz Performance from The New School in NYC, taught combos and classes at the California Jazz Conservatory for nearly a decade, and ran the Berkeley Jazz Workshops — small-group jazz instruction for adult musicians. His own playing has taken him to SFJAZZ, the Telluride Jazz Festival, and Irving Plaza.

Chris Bates

Bass · Co-Founder

Chris is a bassist, composer, and Co-Founder of North Star Jazz Workshops. A vital figure in the Twin Cities jazz scene, he holds a McKnight Composer's Fellowship and has released several critically acclaimed albums, including ongoing projects with The Good Vibes Trio and Atlantis Quartet. His compositions have been performed by big bands and contemporary ensembles, and he's taught at the University of Minnesota Morris.
